# Writing carousels that get read

## Shape of a carousel
1. **Cover (hook):** one promise the reader wants, in as few words as the design allows. Specific beats clever:
   "7 AI tools that save developers 10+ hours a week" beats "AI is changing everything".
2. **Content slides:** one point each. A short title (the point) + one or two lines (why/how). Keep the same
   structure on every content slide so the reader's eye knows where to look.
3. **Last slide (CTA):** recap or payoff + one clear action ("Save this", "Follow @handle for more", "Comment X").
   Mirror the cover's look so the set feels finished.

## Length
- 5–12 slides; 6–8 is the sweet spot for most topics. Don't pad to hit a number, and don't cram two points onto one
  slide.
- Respect each template slot's sample length (`chars` from `get_template`). Headings that run long wrap and push
  other elements; body text that runs long overflows. When in doubt, cut words, not meaning.

## Hooks that work (pick the one that fits the topic and voice)
- **Number + outcome:** "5 habits that doubled my reach in 90 days"
- **Contrarian:** "Posting daily isn't the hard part. Here's what is."
- **Mistake/cost:** "The 3 LinkedIn mistakes that quietly kill your reach"
- **Before/after story:** "Two years ago I was the person this was built for."
- **Direct promise:** "How I learned to code in 6 months — no degree, no bootcamp"

## Voice
- Write like the account owner speaks: first person for personal profiles, "we" for company pages.
- Plain, concrete words; numbers and examples over adjectives. Avoid filler ("In today's fast-paced world"),
  hype words, and stacks of emoji. If the user has examples of their own posts, match their tone.
- Highlight one or two key words per heading with the template's accent (a `<span style="color:#hex">` or `<b>`),
  not whole sentences.

## Post caption (the text above the carousel)
- 1–3 short paragraphs: the hook again in different words, why it matters, what's inside, a light CTA.
- LinkedIn: keep links in a follow-up comment rather than the post. Instagram: a few relevant hashtags at the end.
